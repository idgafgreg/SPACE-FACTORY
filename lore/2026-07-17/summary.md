## Headline takeaways (apply to our game)

1. **The ship should already be a prison before the hive arrives.** Best modern industrial-horror writing treats the workplace itself as the isolation engine — no need for a melodramatic “doors locked” beat. Our broken far-future freighter/factory ship already sells “no exit” if life-support, salvage schedule, and empty decks feel real.
2. **Threat should use factory anatomy.** Biomass / hive horror lands harder when vents, heat, filters, conveyors, and junction rooms become pathways — not just spawn points. That mirrors our scan → extract → move → process loop: the same systems that grow the factory can feed the scare.
3. **Pressure must plug into the production fantasy.** Factorio’s lasting lesson: enemies that ignore the factory feel like a minigame; enemies tied to growth (pollution, expansion, ammo demand) become identity. Hive waves should tax layout, throughput, and prep windows — not replace belt-building with pure combat.
4. **Horror from routine, not spectacle.** Small wrongnesses first (cold bay, sticky clamp, scanner lag, metallic air) beat jump-scare catalogs. Tone target: sad / lonely / fear — quiet industrial dread before the horde.
5. **Modular workplace kits are enough for uncanny.** Real ships/factories are repetitive by design; once players learn the pattern, *violation* of that pattern is the scare. Supports our sector-based teaching and modular machine placement.
