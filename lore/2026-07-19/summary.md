## Headline takeaways (apply to our game)

1. **Pace dread with a soft “director,” not constant combat.** *Alien: Isolation*’s menace gauge and the Dead Space remake’s Intensity Director both treat fear as a roller coaster: proximity / lighting / distant audio raise tension, then the threat *backs off* so the next spike lands. For SPACE FACTORY: between hive waves, use failing lights, vent sounds, and scanner lag — then quiet — so prep windows feel earned, not empty.
2. **The workplace must feel true before it feels haunted.** *Still Wakes the Deep* research (oil-rig blueprints, worker interviews, machines prioritized over people) shows authenticity sells isolation. Our factory ship should read as a labor habitat first: cramped machine decks, lived-in lockers, schedules that assume a crew that never arrives.
3. **Recovery beats keep loneliness readable.** Chinese Room designers deliberately insert pause moments (warming hands, quiet reflection) so players process dread. Factory loop already has “build between waves” — decorate those gaps with sad routine, not just inventory UI.
4. **Factory comps are splitting: descent horror vs flesh-as-industry.** *Substructure* (dig → awaken threats) and *Biofactory* (hive-mind *is* the factory) show the niche is heating up. We stay distinct: steel factory primary, biomass as parasite of *our* logistics — not cartoon organ belts as the player fantasy.
5. **Infection via process systems remains the strongest hive motif.** Fiction leads this run (*The Meeps*, contaminated salvage/exclusion zones) reinforce prior biomass-in-vents notes: mineral slurry, water recycle, filters, heat — the same pipes that keep the factory alive.
