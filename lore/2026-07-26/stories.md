## This Sepulchral Aegis — generation ship as machine mausoleum

- **Source:** Rob Gillham, *This Sepulchral Aegis*, Clarkesworld Issue 231 (December 2025) — https://clarkesworldmagazine.com/gillham_12_25/
- **Motif (summary):** A vast generation ship read as a floating mausoleum — annexes choked with discarded machinery and centuries of bric-a-brac, vaulted dark, cryo rows of desiccated remains, architecture that preserved *death* as obsessively as life. Scale makes any living figure tiny; main approaches stay clear while side bays drown in junk.
- **SPACE FACTORY use:** Environmental storytelling for unused expansion deck and scrap annexes — company clutter as pre-hive hostility (bible: company built the trap first). Empty headroom can feel like a warehouse of abandoned shifts, not a blank grey box. Original dressing only; no prose lift.

## Lonely Ghosts — empty city still finishing its protocol

- **Source:** Meghan Feldman, *Lonely Ghosts*, Clarkesworld Issue 209 (February 2024) — https://clarkesworldmagazine.com/feldman_02_24/
- **Motif (summary):** A city-building machine keeps constructing at high efficiency after Last Contact; the finished megacity waits empty for thousands of years. Protocol outlives purpose. The living become ghosts haunting an architecture that never needed them to keep going.
- **SPACE FACTORY use:** Cheerful shift boards and belt lines that still demand quota after the berths are empty (pairs 07-25 debt-PA). Soft scare: machines that report "nominal" while the crew is gone. Write original company copy — do not quote the story.

## Tone check (shared)

Both pieces reinforce **lonely industrial dread** without chase set pieces: scale + abandoned purpose + systems that continue. Keep sad/fear register; avoid gothic pulp excess and comedy recycling gags (Viscera Cleanup anti-comp still holds from 07-22).
