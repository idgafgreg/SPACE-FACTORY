# Systems & Progression

# Systems Direction

- Primary identity: factory management under recurring threat, not a combat game with automation elements.
- Core pacing: predictable work cycles create planning windows, then defense phases test preparation quality.
- Desired player skill: improve throughput, layout, and readiness between each incoming threat cycle.
- Narrative role: support motivation and mood through logs, sector context, and environmental clues rather than long story interruptions.

# Cycle Loop

- Phase 1 — Work Window: scan for resources, route materials, run machines, place buildings, and prepare defenses before the next cycle arrives.
- Phase 2 — Warning Window: alarms, ship signs, or motion readings announce the next threat with enough lead time for final repositioning and emergency decisions.
- Phase 3 — Defense Window: alien pressure disrupts production lanes, damages structures, threatens chokepoints, and forces the player to stabilize critical operations.
- Phase 4 — Recovery Window: repair damage, clear blockages, restore power or flow, and evaluate what failed before the next work cycle begins.
- Default cadence: one readable threat cycle per in-game day, shift, or operating cycle so players can plan intentionally rather than guess.
- Escalation pattern: early cycles teach the structure, mid cycles increase lane pressure and enemy durability, later cycles introduce multi-point disruption and recovery strain.

# Progression Skeleton

- Starting player capability: basic scanner, manual extraction, one simple processor, basic conveyor routing, and one entry-level defense option.
- Early resource set: one common structural resource, one energy or fuel resource, and one rare component that gates better machines or defenses.
- Early machine chain: extract raw material, move it through transport, refine it, then convert it into build parts, power supply, or ammunition-equivalent support.
- Early defense set: one placeable barrier, one automated or semi-automated turret/trap, and one emergency player-triggered response tool.
- Unlock logic: each progression tier should improve either throughput, resilience, warning quality, or recovery efficiency.
- Tier 1 goal: achieve a stable loop that survives early waves with basic repairs and simple routing.
- Tier 2 goal: reduce manual friction with better transport, stronger chokepoints, and more dependable production uptime.
- Tier 3 goal: operate multiple critical lines at once while containing larger disruptions without total collapse.

# Defense Pressure Model

- Threat purpose: force tradeoffs, expose weak layouts, and create urgency without invalidating long-term planning.
- Pressure target: enemies should threaten routes, power, access, and production continuity more than they threaten constant direct player death.
- Wave readability: each cycle should communicate likely intensity and rough arrival timing so the player can make meaningful preparation choices.
- Failure model: losing a wave should cause damage, lost materials, broken lines, or delayed progression rather than immediate campaign-ending punishment every time.
- Escalation levers: more simultaneous entry points, stronger enemies, longer disruption windows, less safe downtime, and higher repair burden.
- Fairness rule: if a wave destroys key infrastructure, the recovery path must remain understandable and achievable.
- Defense success should come mostly from prior preparation, chokepoint design, and redundancy rather than twitch execution alone.
- Direct combat rule: player weapons and actions should buy time, finish stragglers, and save failing chokepoints, but should not outperform well-built defensive infrastructure.
- Player combat kit should stay small and readable: one basic sidearm or tool, one emergency support action, and repair/reposition actions during pressure windows.

# First Playable Slice

- Map scope: one compact ship sector with a few resource points, a central processing zone, and 2–3 defendable approaches.
- Player objective: establish one functioning production line that can survive the first several cycles and fund basic expansion.
- Minimum content: 3 resource types, 3–4 machines, 2 defense tools, 1 repair action, and 3 escalating threat cycles.
- Validation goal: confirm that optimizing between predictable waves feels satisfying and that defenses support the factory loop rather than overwhelm it.
- Cut if needed: extra sectors, advanced enemy types, deep story beats, and any mechanic that does not improve the core cycle.
- Combat validation goal: verify that direct player action feels helpful in emergencies without replacing the need for planning, chokepoints, and layered defenses.

# Open Design Constraints

- Need naming pass for resources, machines, and threat cycle terminology.
- Approved decision: use light direct combat, with a simple player defense toolset and defense structures remaining the main answer to incoming waves.
- Need clearer rule for whether scanning is a one-time discovery mechanic or a recurring optimization tool.
- Need first pass on failure economy: repair costs, downtime penalties, and recovery pacing.
