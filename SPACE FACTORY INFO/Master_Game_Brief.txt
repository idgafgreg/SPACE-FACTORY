# Master Game Brief

# Game Summary

- One-paragraph concept summary:  
- Why this game should exist:  This is a passion project, and I personally think it might fit a niche

# Core Fantasy

- What the player does and feels at the highest level: Feels satisfying, Overwhelmed, and Fear all at once

# Player Promise

- What experience the player can reliably expect: A balanced mix of factory optimization and defensive pressure, with steady escalation across each cycle and story present mainly through context, atmosphere, and light progression beats.

# Target Audience

- Who the game is for: Adult gamers who like management factory games + horror games
- Who it is not for: children, and people who are close minded 

# Genre and Platform

- Genre and subgenre: management and horror
- Platform assumptions: hopefully steam

# Core Loop

- Primary player loop: scan → extract → move resource → process → build/unlock \*while that's happening they have to defend from random waves of hive aliens
- Short session flow: refine and improve already set up conveyor belts while defending

# Design Pillars

- Pillar 1: Factory management is the primary identity; every major feature should strengthen planning, throughput, and layout optimization.
- Pillar 2: Defensive pressure should regularly disrupt and test the factory without overtaking it as the dominant activity.
- Pillar 3: Story should enrich the setting and player motivation, but remain secondary to atmosphere, systems, and progression.

# Tone and Mood

- Intended emotional tone: Sad, lonely, and fear
- Visual and thematic direction: rustic space worker, far future, broken down ship, zombie like alien enemies (think of horde from Halo and aliens from deadspace)

# Scope Guardrails

- What must stay in scope: Has to stay a factory game with horror annotations 
- What stays out of scope for now: Multiplayer, space walks
- View modes (2026-07-20): dual orbit/iso + first-person, toggleable; factory-primary in both (see lore/BIBLE.md)

# Success Criteria

- What success looks like in planning: The project has a clearly scoped factory-first gameplay structure, predictable cycle-based threat escalation, and a narrative layer that supports rather than drives production decisions.
- What success looks like for players: Players feel steadily more capable at optimizing their ship, preparing for each coming threat cycle, and uncovering just enough story to deepen the mood without interrupting the loop.

# Current Assumptions

- Assumption 1: The best default pacing is a predictable cycle-based escalation model, such as daily or per-shift threat waves, so players can plan around pressure rather than react to pure chaos.
- Assumption 2: Narrative is delivered through environmental context, light progression beats, and atmosphere rather than heavy cutscenes or story-first mission structure.

# Open Approval Questions

- Approved decision: Use a balanced management/defense split, with management remaining the primary activity and defense acting as recurring pressure.
- Approved decision: Use predictable cycle-based escalation and keep story present but secondary to systems, atmosphere, and progression.
