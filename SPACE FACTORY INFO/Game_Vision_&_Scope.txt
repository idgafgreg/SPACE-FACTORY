# Game Vision & Scope

# Vision Statement

- A horror-infused factory management game set aboard a broken-down far-future ship, where the player scans, extracts, processes, and automates resources while surviving pressure from hive-like alien waves.
- The game should stand out by combining the long-form satisfaction of production optimization with the emotional tension of isolation, fear, and constant environmental threat.

# Experience Goals

- Deliver satisfying automation growth without losing moment-to-moment danger.
- Make players feel lonely, pressured, and resourceful rather than comfortable or overpowered.
- Keep the ship itself feeling like both a workplace and a hostile survival space.
- Reward planning and layout refinement under pressure, not only combat reactions.

# Audience and Positioning

- Primary audience: adult players who enjoy management, factory-building, optimization, and horror tension.
- Likely appeal: players who enjoy optimization games but want stronger atmosphere, dread, and defensive pressure than standard factory sims provide.
- Not aimed at: younger audiences, players seeking low-stress cozy automation, or players who only want pure action horror without systems depth.

# Must Have Features

- Resource scanning and extraction.
- Conveyor or transport-based resource movement.
- Processing chains that convert raw materials into useful outputs.
- Build and unlock progression tied to industrial expansion.
- Defensive response to random or semi-random alien wave threats.
- Layout refinement during short play sessions.

# Nice to Have Features

- Environmental storytelling that deepens the lonely industrial-horror mood.
- Expanded ship sectors or distinct processing zones once the core loop is stable.
- More enemy variants and threat behaviors after the baseline defense loop is proven.

# Excluded Features

- Multiplayer.
- Space-walk systems and external EVA gameplay.
- Feature creep that turns the game into a broad survival sandbox instead of a focused factory-horror experience.

# View modes (human decision 2026-07-20)

- Dual-mode: orbit/iso and first-person, both shipped and toggleable.
- Factory layout/throughput remains the primary skill expression in both modes — FP must not become a shooter.
- Iso stays fully playable; default view mode does not change until FP clears the Wave 1 playtest gate (see `BACKLOG.md` F1–F14 and `lore/BIBLE.md`).

# Deck footprint (human decision 2026-07-21)

- Playable map size stays (~120×80). Early empty deck is expansion headroom, not a bug.
- Fill emptiness by growing the factory (machines, belts, lanes, progress-scaled dressing) — do not shrink walls or the playable area. See `lore/BIBLE.md`.

# Scope Tiers

- MVP: one strong resource pipeline, one defendable ship environment, a small set of processing machines, a limited unlock tree, and recurring alien wave pressure.
- Target Version: broader factory complexity, clearer progression beats, more ship sectors, and more varied threats.
- Future Expansion: deeper narrative arcs, higher-tier automation, and expanded enemy ecology if the base game loop proves strong.

# Feature Fit Checks

- Does this feature strengthen factory optimization under pressure?
- Does it increase dread, isolation, or threat without replacing the management loop?
- Can it be explained and learned without bloating the early game?
- Would removing it make the game more focused?

# Core Loop Breakdown

- Scan ship areas or resource nodes to identify valuable materials.
- Extract and transport materials through a conveyor-driven or equivalent logistics chain.
- Process outputs into upgrades, construction parts, or survival-critical supplies.
- Use outputs to expand, reinforce, or optimize the ship’s industrial layout.
- Defend the operation from alien wave disruptions and recover from damage or breakdowns.
- Repeat with more efficiency, higher throughput, and rising danger.

# Pillar Compliance Notes

- Factory management remains the lead identity; horror should intensify the loop, not replace it.
- Combat and defense should create pressure and interruption, not dominate session time.
- Atmosphere should support loneliness, dread, and industrial decay across art, sound, and pacing.
