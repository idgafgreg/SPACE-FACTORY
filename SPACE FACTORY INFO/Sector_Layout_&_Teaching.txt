# Sector Layout & Teaching

# Sector Purpose

- This first sector should prove that factory planning, readable wave pressure, and light direct combat work together in one compact space.
- The map should be small enough to understand quickly, but large enough to create routing choices and at least two meaningful defensive decisions.
- The player should finish the slice understanding the full rhythm: gather, build, fortify, survive, repair, and expand.

# Layout Zones

- Zone A — Command Hub: starting safe zone with player spawn, basic build access, repair access, and enough open space to teach first placements.
- Zone B — Scrap Vein: nearest low-risk resource source used to teach extraction and first transport routing.
- Zone C — Power Nook: secondary resource or energy source placed just far enough away to force a simple expansion choice.
- Zone D — Processing Deck: central area where the first core machine chain is expected to be built and visually read by the player.
- Zone E — West Access Corridor: narrow defendable lane with clear chokepoint potential, used to teach barrier placement.
- Zone F — Vent Breach Approach: second threat lane with a less obvious path, used to teach that one defense line is not enough.
- Zone G — Maintenance Pocket: fallback area for repairs and repositioning, protected enough to support recovery but not fully immune to failure spillover.

# Interior Volume (F6, 2026-07-21)

- The deck is enclosed. Ceiling underside sits at y = 3.2 against wall tops at ~2.9, giving roughly
  1.55 of headroom over a 1.65 eye — industrial-cramped rather than warehouse. **F13 audited this
  (2026-07-27) and the volume PASSES — see "Human-Scale Audit" below. No wall, ceiling or gate
  geometry was changed.**
- Coverage spans the whole walkable deck (x ±60, z ±40), not just the authored hull (x ±42.5,
  z ±24.5). The edge rails from P2 sit at the Ground lip, so the player can stand well outside the
  hull; a lid that stopped at the hull left a band to walk into and look up at open sky from.
- The ceiling is built at runtime by ShipInteriorUpgrade and is visible in first person only.
  CeilingVisibility switches it off in iso, where an overhead camera would otherwise be looking at
  the inside of a lid. Neither view mode is allowed to regress the other.
- Panels do not cast shadows and are culled from point lights (the A5 wall-cap layer trick).
  Corridor lamps hang under a metre below the ceiling and would blow it out to white, and letting a
  solid lid occlude A8's 0.18 rim sun would darken the whole deck and put A8b threat readability at
  risk. F7 owns re-lighting the ceiling deliberately once fixtures are mounted to it.

# Corridor Lighting (F7, 2026-07-21)

- Corridor lamps are real fixtures: stem, housing and lens bolted to the ceiling. Every third lamp
  is dead (A8's rule, unchanged) but a dead lamp is now a cold dark housing rather than nothing,
  because at eye level an empty gap reads as a missing asset while a failed fixture reads as
  neglect. 15 fixtures: 10 live, 5 dead.
- Light values are per view mode. Iso keeps A8 exactly — source at y 2.35, range 9, intensity 1.5 —
  and the housings are hidden there, so the overhead frame is byte-for-byte what A8 signed off.
  First person moves the source to y 2.95 (just under the ceiling, 1.30 above a 1.65 eye), range 11,
  intensity 2.2.
- Flicker and the AlarmLevel coupling are unchanged. Per-mode intensity goes through
  LampFlicker.SetBaseIntensity so the flicker modulates around the active value.
- OPEN: eye-level corridors are not yet readable, and lamp values cannot fix it. Measured — sweeping
  intensity 1.5 to 8 moves frame mean luma 0.024 to 0.033, while disabling post-processing on the
  same frame moves it to 0.134. The A1 grade removes 82% of the eye-level image because it was tuned
  against the iso frame. F8 owns the per-mode grade/ambient and should re-check the lamp values
  above once it lands.

# Resource Node Readability (Wave C, 2026-07-21)

- Resource nodes render as a cluster of resource-coloured emissive crystals on the plinth
  (NodeReadabilityMarker). The generic art backfill collapses a node's fitted placeholder to a
  ~0.14m speck, so without this a node read as a bare dark disc — a first-person playtest could not
  tell a node from the floor. The cluster is the readable element; the plinth is just its base.
- Colour signals the resource: scrap = amber, energy = yellow, circuits = blue, advanced = violet.
  Emission is deliberately low (below the bloom threshold) so the crystals read as faceted geometry
  rather than a glow blob, with a small point light so the deposit is findable in the dark.
- Shard renderers carry "Readability" in their name so RuntimeArtBackfill's HideHostRenderers
  exempts them on its rescan (same mechanism the plinth uses); otherwise the rescan blanks them.

# Flow Through The Sector

- Start at the Command Hub with immediate visual access to the nearest scrap source and the central processing space.
- The first natural route should be short and readable: extract scrap, move it to the Processing Deck, and convert it into build parts.
- The second route should ask the player to reach the Power Nook, adding a mild extension problem before heavier defense pressure begins.
- Threat lanes should intersect the edges of production space indirectly, so bad planning hurts operations without constant unavoidable player interruption.
- Movement loops should allow the player to check extraction, production, and both defense lanes in a fast patrol path.

# Defense Lanes

- Lane 1 — West Corridor: primary early wave lane, narrow enough that a barrier plus one turret/trap creates an understandable first defensive success.
- Lane 2 — Vent Breach: secondary lane introduced after the first defense success so players learn to avoid overcommitting to one chokepoint.
- Lane pressure should first threaten access and machine uptime, then later threaten simultaneous response capacity.
- Player direct combat should matter most when leaks get through, when a turret is underperforming, or when repairs must be protected.
- No lane should require perfect aim or constant manual fighting; good structure should still be the dominant solution.

# Teaching Flow

## Cycle 1 — Establish The Loop

- Teach the nearest resource loop first: collect scrap, place the first extractor, route output, and build one processor.
- Give the player enough quiet time to understand placement and throughput before any serious threat arrives.
- First wave uses only the West Corridor and is weak enough that one simple defense line plus light player support can hold.
- Post-wave recovery should be brief and readable, reinforcing that defense is part of the production rhythm.

## Cycle 2 — Expand And Fortify

- Introduce the Power Nook so the player must extend infrastructure and decide how much to invest in economy versus defense.
- Second wave remains manageable but forces stronger use of barriers, better turret placement, or more efficient patrol movement.
- The player should feel the cost of weak preparation through downtime or damage, not instant failure.
- Recovery after cycle 2 should teach repair triage: restore the line that protects future stability first.

## Cycle 3 — Split Attention Under Pressure

- Introduce the Vent Breach as a second active pressure source so the player must split attention across economy, lane defense, and quick intervention.
- This wave should create one likely leak scenario that rewards direct player cleanup without making manual combat the dominant strategy.
- A successful player should end cycle 3 with a stable basic factory, two viable defensive responses, and a clear sense of what better automation would solve next.
- Failure in cycle 3 should still allow recovery if the Command Hub and one resource line remain intact.

## Teaching Rules

- Only teach one new mental model at a time whenever possible.
- Use map geometry to suggest good first solutions before adding explicit instruction.
- Every wave should teach something about preparation, not just survival.
- If a player fails, the layout should make the cause legible: weak lane coverage, overextended routing, or delayed repairs.

# Failure And Recovery

- Primary recoverable state: lose a lane, suffer machine damage, spend time and materials to restore operations, then continue.
- Soft fail state: production stalls because one critical route is damaged or underpowered, forcing repair prioritization.
- Hard fail state for the slice: Command Hub overrun or total loss of both production and defensive continuity.
- Recovery materials should be attainable inside the same sector so a damaged run is not automatically dead.

# Prototype Validation

- Can a new player establish the first functioning production loop without confusion?
- Can the player identify at least one strong chokepoint naturally?
- Does wave 3 create tension without forcing pure manual combat?
- After failure, does the player understand what to change in layout or priorities?
- Does the sector end with a clear desire for more automation, stronger defenses, or better expansion tools?

# Prototype Spec

## Player Starting Kit

- Basic sidearm: low damage, short range, reliable, intended for finishing leaks and defending repairs rather than solving full waves alone.
- Repair tool: restores structure and machine health over time, forcing the player to commit attention during recovery windows.
- Build tool: places and removes prototype structures and allows repositioning of a limited subset of defenses between waves.
- Emergency support action: short cooldown overdrive burst that temporarily boosts one nearby defense or machine, used as a clutch intervention tool.
- Starting inventory should be enough for one extractor, one processor, one basic power connection, two barriers, and one turret so the first good plan is achievable immediately.

## Machine List

- Scrap Extractor: harvests the nearest scrap vein and establishes the first resource loop.
- Power Tap: connects the Power Nook resource to the local grid and acts as the first expansion requirement.
- Processor: converts raw scrap into build parts needed for defenses, repairs, and expansion.
- Relay Node: extends logistics or power connection across short distances and creates the first layout efficiency decisions.
- Stockpile Bin: buffers a small amount of parts so the player can absorb brief production disruption without total collapse.
- No advanced splitters, crafting trees, or automation scripting in this slice; the machine set should stay intentionally small.

## Defense List

- Barrier: cheap lane-shaping structure with moderate health, mainly used to create or strengthen chokepoints.
- Auto Turret: short-to-mid range direct-fire defense with clear line-of-sight needs and low enough damage that placement matters.
- Shock Trap: single-lane support defense with cooldown-based area control, good for buying time rather than killing everything.
- Repair Post: optional support structure that speeds nearby repairs and encourages fallback stabilization after a wave.
- Only one offensive tower type in the prototype. Do not add sniper towers, missile towers, or drones yet.

## Wave Timings And Pressure Plan

- Prep lock (Decision 2026-07-22 / playtest 2026-07-15): **Wave 1 setup = 40 seconds**; **all later preps = 30 seconds**. Do not use multi-minute prep windows.
- Cycle 1 setup (40s): place the first extractor, first processor, and first lane defense under short pressure — teaching is tight, not leisurely.
- Wave 1: 60 seconds from the West Corridor only. Composition should be light enough that a basic barrier line, one turret, and minor player support succeed.
- Between-wave prep (30s): repair, reload defenses if relevant, one meaningful layout correction — feel time pressure.
- Wave 2: 75 seconds, mostly West Corridor with a small side-pressure hint from the Vent Breach approach or pre-wave warning signs there.
- Wave 3: 90 seconds. West Corridor remains the main lane while Vent Breach becomes active enough to create likely leaks or repair interruptions. Use the 30s prep before W3 to finalize a second defensive answer.
- After Wave 3 the run continues: waves 1-3 are the teaching arc, not the end of the run.
- Target length for the teaching arc (cycles 1-3): much shorter than the old multi-minute prep plan — expect a brisk first successful teaching pass.

## Run Structure And Fail Conditions

- No win condition: the run is an infinite loop of escalating cycles. Surviving waves 1-3 proves the player has learned the rhythm; the game then keeps generating harder cycles indefinitely.
- Long-term motivation comes from lots of progression: unlocking new machines, defenses, and upgrades, and expanding the factory and defense network across cycles.
- Secondary success condition: maintain at least one active extractor and one active processor, proving economy continuity.
- Hard fail if the Command Hub is destroyed or disabled beyond recovery.
- Hard fail if both core production functions are lost at once: no active extraction and no active processing with no recovery path.
- Soft fail state should exist inside the run whenever defense collapse causes long production downtime, but the player can still salvage the sector.
- Prototype scoring, medals, or grading should be excluded for now. Focus only on comprehension, tension, and recoverability.

## Prototype Scope Guardrails

- No hero abilities beyond the single emergency overdrive action.
- No more than five machine types and four defense/support structures in the prototype.
- Only two active enemy lanes in the slice.
- Keep enemy variety low enough that lane behavior is readable and counterplay comes mostly from placement and timing.
- If the prototype becomes fun only when adding more weapons or more tower types, the core loop is not solved yet.

## Enemy Roster And Wave Composition

## Enemy Roster

- Crawler: baseline enemy. Fast, fragile, attacks the nearest valid structure or player. Its job is to test lane coverage and punish leaks, not soak damage.
- Bruiser: slow, durable lane-breaker. Prioritizes barriers and front-line defenses. Its job is to punish shallow chokepoints and create windows for smaller enemies to slip through.
- Sapper: medium-speed disruption unit. Prefers power-facing or support structures when it can path to them. Its job is to create repair pressure and make the second lane tactically relevant.
- No ranged enemies, no flyers, and no stealth units in this first slice. Readability matters more than variety.

## Targeting Rules

- Default rule: enemies attack the first blocking defense or the nearest active structure along their path.
- Crawlers switch to the player if the player is nearby and damaging them, reinforcing active cleanup without letting the player kite the whole lane forever.
- Bruisers strongly prefer barriers, turrets, and the Command Hub if the lane is open.
- Sappers prefer Relay Nodes, Power Taps, Repair Posts, and lightly protected processing-side structures over pure front-line targets when a valid route exists.
- Targeting should be readable enough that players understand why a breach hurt their economy.

## Rough Enemy Prototype Stats

- Tuning baseline: assume one Auto Turret is the core reference defense. These values should make one turret plus a decent chokepoint handle light pressure, but not solve every wave without support.

## Crawler

- HP: 35
- Move speed: 1.4 tiles per second relative prototype pace. It should feel clearly faster than the player’s comfortable repair rhythm.
- Structure damage: 6 per hit
- Attack interval: 1.0 second
- Player damage: light. Enough that two or three Crawlers become dangerous if ignored at close range, but one leak is recoverable.
- Target priority: nearest blocker first, then nearest active structure, then player if the player is within local aggro range and actively interfering.
- Role intent: punish uncovered lanes and poor cleanup, not erase sturdy defenses by itself.

## Bruiser

- HP: 140
- Move speed: 0.7 tiles per second. Slow enough to telegraph threat, fast enough that the player cannot ignore one forever.
- Structure damage: 18 per hit
- Attack interval: 1.5 seconds
- Player damage: moderate. Dangerous in melee, especially if the player is already occupied by smaller leaks.
- Target priority: barriers first, then turrets and Repair Posts, then Command Hub if the lane is open.
- Role intent: force layered defense and punish shallow builds that rely on one fragile front line.

## Sapper

- HP: 55
- Move speed: 1.0 tiles per second. Fast enough to exploit weak backlines, but not so fast that it feels impossible to intercept.
- Structure damage: 10 per hit baseline, but apply a disruption bonus against power or relay structures if the prototype supports subsystem vulnerability.
- Attack interval: 0.9 seconds
- Player damage: light to moderate. The threat is positional disruption more than direct dueling power.
- Target priority: Relay Nodes and Power Taps first, then Repair Posts, then Processors and Stockpile Bins, then any nearby fallback structure.
- Role intent: create backline panic and force the player to protect infrastructure, not just the obvious chokepoint.

## Tuning Notes

- These values are intentionally readable ratios: Bruiser has about four times Crawler HP, Sapper has slightly more than one and a half times Crawler HP.
- If Crawlers overwhelm too quickly, reduce speed before reducing count. Their identity depends on movement pressure.
- If Bruisers feel irrelevant, raise barrier damage slightly before adding HP. They should be scary because they break plans, not because they take forever to kill.
- If Sappers feel unfair, simplify target logic before lowering numbers. Confusing behavior is more harmful than strong behavior.
- The player should lose runs mainly because the defense layout was incomplete or recovery was mismanaged, not because enemy raw stats were overtuned.

## Rough Defense Prototype Stats

- Tuning baseline: the defense network should carry most of the survival burden. Player intervention should stabilize mistakes, not replace planning.

## Barrier

- Health: 180
- Purpose: buy firing time, absorb Bruiser pressure, and let one turret reach expected value.
- Performance check: one Barrier should comfortably survive a small Crawler pack long enough for a turret to thin it, but should visibly fail if two Bruisers sit on it unsupported.

## Auto Turret

- DPS: 22 sustained single-target damage
- Range: 6 tiles
- Target rule: nearest enemy in lane by default, with no complex priority logic in the first sector.
- Purpose: one turret plus one barrier should solve Wave 1 if placed well, but should not fully solve Wave 2 or Wave 3 without support, repairs, or a second angle.

## Shock Trap

- Cooldown: 10 seconds between activations
- Purpose: tempo control, not main damage. It should create breathing room for repairs and turret uptime.
- Performance check: one trap trigger should noticeably blunt a Crawler pulse, but should only soften a Bruiser-led push rather than nullify it.

## Repair Tool And Repair Post

- Player repair speed: 28 health per second while channeling
- Repair Post passive repair: 8 health per second to its linked local structure or zone target
- Purpose: player repair is a clutch stabilization tool; Repair Post is long-form sustain that rewards setup.
- Performance check: a player actively repairing can keep one damaged barrier alive through light pressure or briefly extend it through one Bruiser, but cannot out-repair two Bruisers and a leak pack alone.

## Command Hub

- Durability: 500
- Purpose: losing the hub should signal multiple compounding failures, not a single accidental leak.
- Performance check: several Crawlers getting through is painful but not instantly run-ending; repeated Bruiser access or ignored split-lane collapse should be fatal.

## Expected Outcome Checks

- Wave 1 check: one Barrier plus one Auto Turret should beat the opening Crawler groups with minor repair input and survive the final Bruiser if the player helps.
- Wave 2 check: one lane setup should begin to crack unless supported by repairs, trap timing, or stronger placement. This is desired.
- Wave 3 check: a player who only built front-line damage should suffer support-line losses once Sappers arrive. This is desired.
- If Wave 1 feels too harsh, raise Barrier health slightly or reduce Bruiser structure damage before buffing turret DPS.
- If Wave 3 feels too easy, improve Sapper access or reduce repair safety before increasing raw enemy HP.

## Paper Balance Pass And Player Combat Values

- Balance assumption: use one solid lane anchor as the baseline defense package unless otherwise noted: 1 Barrier, 1 Auto Turret, and player intervention as needed.

## Wave 1 Paper Pass

- Expected composition intent: mostly Crawlers with one Bruiser check near the end.
- Turret output check: 22 DPS kills one Crawler in about 1.6 seconds and one Bruiser in about 6.4 seconds if uninterrupted.
- Barrier soak check: one Bruiser dealing 18 every 1.5 seconds removes a 180 health Barrier in about 15 seconds if not repaired.
- Practical outcome: one Barrier plus one Turret should comfortably process early Crawlers. When the Bruiser arrives, the player can finish the check by repairing the Barrier, body-blocking briefly, or adding sidearm damage.
- Desired lesson: chokepoint plus baseline firepower works, but unattended melee pressure is still meaningful.

## Wave 2 Paper Pass

- Expected composition intent: thicker Crawler pressure with sustained contact time and at least one Bruiser-backed push.
- Stress check: a Turret alone cannot kill a full mixed push before contact if lane length is moderate. That is good. The player must either preserve barrier life, time a trap, or add a second support element.
- Repair check: player repair at 28 HP per second can erase one Bruiser’s 12 DPS equivalent pressure, but cannot keep up with a Bruiser plus concurrent Crawler chip plus positional distractions forever.
- Practical outcome: shallow one-lane plans begin to crack. Good builds survive by layering time-buying pieces rather than by expecting one gun to erase everything.
- Desired lesson: defense quality and timing matter more than frantic direct combat.

## Wave 3 Paper Pass

- Expected composition intent: split attention test. Frontline still matters, but Sappers should force infrastructure coverage.
- Sapper check: at 55 HP, a Turret kills one Sapper in about 2.5 seconds if it gets clear targeting, but the default nearest-target rule means Sappers can still slip value through mixed pressure. That is good.
- Support-line fragility check: Repair Post passive sustain at 8 HP per second is not enough to ignore Sapper hits. Infrastructure must be screened, repositioned, or actively defended.
- Practical outcome: players who invested only in the obvious front door should experience functional collapse in support systems before full base death. That is the right escalation.
- Desired lesson: complete layouts beat brute-force lane DPS.

## Player Combat Values

- Sidearm damage: 14 per shot
- Fire rate: 2 shots per second
- Sustained player DPS: 28 if uninterrupted, roughly comparable to one turret but with heavy opportunity cost because shooting means not repairing or repositioning.
- Range: 4.5 tiles, shorter than turret range so the player must step into danger to contribute damage.
- Reload or heat rule: after 12 shots, sidearm pauses for 1.2 seconds. This prevents perfect solo carry uptime.
- Player health baseline: 120
- Player movement baseline: 1.15 tiles per second. Faster than Bruisers, slightly faster than Sappers, but not enough to casually answer every lane problem.
- Design intent: the player can finish weakened targets, rescue a failing choke, or pick off a Sapper, but cannot replace missing coverage or poor infrastructure planning.

## Likely Failure Cases

## Leak Overwhelm

- Cause: too little lane coverage, overreliance on one turret, or trap timing wasted early.
- Player-facing outcome: multiple Crawlers slip through, forcing the player to chase instead of maintain the layout.
- Lesson taught: coverage and pacing matter more than panic shooting.

## Barrier Collapse

- Cause: Bruiser contact left unchecked, no layered blockers, or the player commits to DPS instead of preserving time-buying structures.
- Player-facing outcome: the frontline opens suddenly and every following enemy gains efficient pathing.
- Lesson taught: barriers are time assets that must be protected, not disposable decorations.

## Backline Disruption

- Cause: Sappers reach relay, repair, or economy pieces because all investment was pointed at the frontline.
- Player-facing outcome: the base still looks alive, but repair throughput, power routing, or production reliability starts failing.
- Lesson taught: support infrastructure is part of the defense puzzle.

## Hub Attrition

- Cause: repeated leaks, failed recovery after barrier collapse, or ignoring split pressure too long.
- Player-facing outcome: the player loses from accumulated strategic failure rather than a single surprise event.
- Lesson taught: recovery windows exist, but they must be used early.

## Recommended Tuning Reactions

- If players are winning Wave 3 by pure sidearm play, reduce sidearm uptime or increase repair opportunity cost before buffing enemies.
- If players never feel pressure from Sappers, improve approach access or support-line vulnerability before raising Sapper HP.
- If frontline defense collapses too abruptly, slightly lengthen Barrier survival windows before increasing passive repair.

## Locked Wave 1 To Wave 3 Compositions

- Composition goal: Wave 1 proves baseline lane defense, Wave 2 cracks shallow plans, Wave 3 tests support coverage and recovery discipline.

## Wave 1

- Purpose: teach the player that one competent choke plus light intervention is enough.
- Spawn structure: 3 pulses over 32 seconds.
- Pulse A at 0s: 4 Crawlers on main lane.
- Pulse B at 10s: 5 Crawlers on main lane.
- Pulse C at 22s: 1 Bruiser plus 3 Crawlers on main lane.
- Expected player answer: 1 Barrier plus 1 Turret handles most pressure; the player repairs or sidearms during the Bruiser pulse.
- Failure signal if overtuned: the player loses the choke before the Bruiser arrives or must personally clear most Crawlers.

## Wave 2

- Purpose: punish one-dimensional lane setups and reward layering, trap timing, and repair prioritization.
- Spawn structure: 4 pulses over 42 seconds.
- Pulse A at 0s: 5 Crawlers on main lane.
- Pulse B at 8s: 4 Crawlers on main lane plus 2 Crawlers on side lane.
- Pulse C at 20s: 1 Bruiser plus 4 Crawlers on main lane.
- Pulse D at 31s: 1 Bruiser on side lane plus 3 Crawlers split between lanes.
- Expected player answer: one strong lane is no longer enough. The player should need a trap or second defense angle while using repairs to prevent collapse during Bruiser contact.
- Failure signal if undertuned: a single front choke with no side coverage clears comfortably without consuming player attention.

## Wave 3

- Purpose: force full-layout thinking, punish exposed support lines, and test whether the player can recover from partial disruption.
- Spawn structure: 5 pulses over 55 seconds.
- Pulse A at 0s: 4 Crawlers on main lane and 2 Crawlers on side lane.
- Pulse B at 12s: 1 Sapper plus 3 Crawlers on side lane.
- Pulse C at 24s: 1 Bruiser plus 4 Crawlers on main lane.
- Pulse D at 34s: 1 Sapper on support access route plus 2 Crawlers on side lane.
- Pulse E at 43s: 1 Bruiser on main lane plus 5 Crawlers split across both lanes.
- Expected player answer: the player must rotate between frontline stabilization and backline interception. Pure front-DPS builds should suffer support damage before the wave ends.
- Failure signal if overtuned: one early Sapper hit causes an unrecoverable spiral before the player can meaningfully respond.

## Cross-Wave Validation Checks

- Wave 1 should be solvable with the minimum intended defense package and light player support.
- Wave 2 should force either secondary coverage, trap discipline, or active repair triage.
- Wave 3 should create recoverable disruption first and lethal hub pressure second.
- If these waves validate cleanly, the next tuning layer is economy pacing: starting resources, build costs, and between-wave recovery time.

## Locked Economy Pacing Package

- Economy goal: the player can afford the intended answer to each wave, but cannot safely overbuild every problem at once.

## Starting Economy

- Starting scrap: 140
- Pre-wave setup window before Wave 1: 40 seconds; all later preps: 30 seconds (Decision 2026-07-22; playtest 2026-07-15)
- Mid-prep menace curve (ThreatTelegraph, lore Intensity Director / Isolation menace): during Prep above the final warning window, schedule 1 dread beat on ~30s preps (2 on longer), peak AlarmLevel ≈0.32 with skitter + soft flash, then a mandatory ~5s quiet valley, then the existing final ~10s warning telegraph. Prep stays plan-able — beats are texture, not combat.
- Intended minimum opening package: 1 Barrier for 30 plus 1 Auto Turret for 80, leaving 30 scrap for either one low-cost utility piece or reserve flexibility.
- Design intent: the player starts with a strong enough core to solve Wave 1, but not enough surplus to trivialize both lanes immediately.

## Structure Costs

- Barrier: 30 scrap
- Auto Turret: 80 scrap
- Shock Trap: 35 scrap
- Repair Post: 60 scrap
- Relay Node: 45 scrap
- Power Tap: 25 scrap
- Processor: 70 scrap
- Stockpile: 50 scrap

## Build Time And Placement Rules

- Barrier build time: 3 seconds
- Auto Turret build time: 6 seconds
- Shock Trap build time: 2 seconds
- Repair Post build time: 5 seconds
- Relay Node build time: 4 seconds
- Power Tap build time: 2 seconds
- Sell refund: 70 percent of paid cost between waves only
- Reposition rule: no free reposition during active waves; rebuilding under pressure is allowed but consumes time and full cost unless the old structure is sold during downtime.

## Wave Income And Recovery

- Wave 1 clear reward: 70 scrap
- Wave 2 clear reward: 85 scrap
- Per-enemy salvage: Crawlers 4 scrap, Bruisers 12 scrap, Sappers 10 scrap
- Between Wave 1 and Wave 2 downtime: 20 seconds
- Between Wave 2 and Wave 3 downtime: 25 seconds
- Repair costs no scrap but consumes the player’s time and attention budget.
- Design intent: rewards plus salvage should let players add one meaningful new answer between waves, not fully solve every future threat.

## Affordability Checks Against Waves 1 To 3

- Before Wave 1, the player can afford Barrier plus Turret and still retain 30 scrap. That is enough for flexibility, but not enough for a second Turret.
- A clean Wave 1 clear yields 70 reward plus 48 salvage from 12 Crawlers and 1 Bruiser, for about 118 new scrap before considering losses.
- That means the Wave 2 prep state can realistically add one Turret or one Repair Post plus one Trap while still preserving some reserve for lane correction.
- A clean Wave 2 clear yields 85 reward plus 72 salvage from 18 Crawlers and 2 Bruisers, for about 157 new scrap before considering losses.
- That gives enough room to prepare real Wave 3 answers, but only if the player did not overbuy weak structures or suffer heavy rebuild tax.
- Conclusion: the intended path is affordable, but overbuilding early should reduce Wave 3 resilience. That is the correct pressure curve.

## Tuning Guardrails

- If testers can open with two Turrets plus stable front cover, starting scrap is too high or Turret cost is too low.
- If Wave 2 cannot be stabilized after a strong Wave 1 clear, either Wave 1 rewards are too low or side-lane answer costs are too high.
- If Wave 3 is only survivable after near-perfect salvage retention, rebuild tax is too punishing for the intended difficulty band.
- Default tuning bias: preserve meaningful scarcity rather than comfort. The player should feel one layer short of total security.

## Locked Minimum Intended Build States

- Purpose: define the leanest build states that should still solve the locked waves if the player reads the level correctly and executes with reasonable discipline.

## Before Wave 1

- 1 Barrier on the main choke
- 1 Auto Turret positioned to cover the same choke but not so far forward that Bruiser contact disables it immediately
- 30 scrap held in reserve or spent on one low-cost utility choice only if it does not remove the need for player intervention
- Expected player behavior: maintain the choke, repair during lulls, and personally stabilize the Bruiser pulse rather than expecting full automation
- Lesson delivered: one competent lane package plus light player support is enough when the threat is legible and concentrated

## Before Wave 2

- Retain the Wave 1 main choke package unless it suffered catastrophic placement failure
- Add 1 Shock Trap on the side lane or just behind the main choke to catch split pressure and buy repair time
- Add either 1 second Barrier on the side lane or 1 Repair Post supporting the main choke, but not both in the minimum state
- Keep at least a small scrap reserve rather than spending to zero; the minimum intended state assumes some correction budget remains
- Expected player behavior: rotate attention between lanes, trigger trap value deliberately, and prevent side pressure from becoming a front-line collapse
- Lesson delivered: shallow expansion is not enough; the player must buy time somewhere and protect the original lane plan

## Before Wave 3

- Retain the Wave 2 main choke package and at least one side-lane answer
- Add 1 Repair Post if it was skipped before, or add 1 Relay Node if the scenario uses support-chain exposure as the Sapper’s target logic
- Add 1 localized backline protection piece: either a Trap near support access or a Barrier that forces the Sapper to path through player-controlled space
- Do not assume a second Auto Turret in the minimum intended state unless testing shows the economy is too tight without it
- Expected player behavior: intercept or delay Sapper pressure, keep the front choke from buckling during Bruiser contact, and recover from one localized disruption without spiraling
- Lesson delivered: support infrastructure now matters, and resilient coverage beats raw front-loaded DPS

## Interpretation Rules

- Minimum intended means the leanest setup that should pass with competent play, not the safest setup or the most comfortable one
- If a wave only works when the player preserves every structure at full health, the minimum state is too fragile
- If a wave is comfortable with no meaningful player movement or repair load, the minimum state is too strong
- The intended test standard is success with pressure, not success with full security

## Validation Questions

- Can a player who follows the minimum Wave 1 state survive without adding a second lane answer too early?
- Does the minimum Wave 2 state force meaningful attention splitting without demanding impossible map coverage?
- Does the minimum Wave 3 state survive one mistake and one partial support disruption, but not repeated neglect?
- If not, is the issue economy scarcity, wave composition, or structure effectiveness?

## Locked Support Structure Durability Package

- Purpose: define how much disruption support pieces can absorb, how quickly the player can recover them, and what level of loss is still survivable in the Wave 3 minimum state.

## Durability Values

- Barrier health: 240
- Auto Turret health: 160
- Shock Trap health: 70
- Repair Post health: 120
- Relay Node health: 90
- Power Tap health: 80
- Processor health: 100
- Stockpile health: 110
- Durability intent: support pieces should die faster than front-line structures, but not so fast that a single brief breach guarantees collapse.

## Repair Rules

- Manual repair rate: 40 health per second while actively repairing
- Repair interruption rule: taking damage or moving cancels repair immediately
- Repair Post passive field: restores nearby structures at 8 health per second while powered and not disabled
- A structure below 25 percent health is considered unstable and should not be assumed to survive another focused hit sequence without intervention
- Repairing a destroyed structure is impossible; it must be rebuilt at full scrap cost and full build time

## Destruction Consequences

- Destroyed Relay Node: linked support effects in its chain go offline until the node is rebuilt
- Destroyed Power Tap: any connected support piece requiring local power is disabled until power is restored
- Destroyed Repair Post: passive sustain is lost immediately, but existing front-line structures remain otherwise functional
- Destroyed Stockpile: stored reserve capacity is lost, but current carried scrap remains untouched
- Destruction intent: support losses should create a recovery problem, not an instant fail state, unless the player was already overextended

## Wave 3 Recovery Thresholds

- Wave 3 minimum state should survive one damaged support piece that remains repairable without forcing a full defensive collapse
- Wave 3 minimum state may survive one destroyed minor support piece such as a Trap or Power Tap, but should become significantly less stable afterward
- Wave 3 minimum state should not reliably survive simultaneous destruction of a Relay Node and Repair Post unless the player had surplus investment beyond the minimum state
- A competent player should be able to restore one damaged key support piece during a short pressure break, but not rebuild an entire broken support chain before the next major contact
- Pass condition for durability tuning: one disruption is recoverable, two overlapping support failures create a probable but not always immediate loss spiral

## Tuning Triggers

- If support pieces die before the player can reasonably see and respond to the threat, raise health or reduce burst access
- If support disruption barely matters and front-line firepower still solves the wave comfortably, lower support health or increase consequence severity
- If rebuilding a lost support chain is mandatory for any chance of survival during the same wave, either downtime is too short or support dependency is too binary
- Default tuning bias: support disruption should force triage and rerouting, not produce unavoidable instant defeat from one hidden failure

## Wave 3 Disruption Paper Simulation

- Simulation baseline: use the locked minimum Wave 3 state, current durability package, predictable wave escalation, and a competent player who rotates between front choke and threatened support space but does not have surplus defenses beyond the minimum.

## Baseline Build State

- Main choke retained: 1 Barrier plus 1 Auto Turret
- Side-lane answer retained: 1 Shock Trap and either 1 side Barrier or 1 Repair Post from prior wave; Wave 3 minimum assumes at least one side answer remains active
- Wave 3 addition retained: 1 Repair Post if absent before, or 1 Relay Node if support-chain exposure is active, plus 1 localized backline protection piece
- No second Auto Turret assumed in the minimum state
- Economy assumption: the player has enough scrap for one meaningful correction or rebuild choice during Wave 3 pressure, but not enough to replace multiple lost support pieces and front-line losses in the same contact window

## Scenario A: One Damaged Key Support Piece

- Test event: a Sapper or leak reaches one key support piece and leaves it damaged but not destroyed, typically at 25 to 45 percent remaining health
- Player response: disengage briefly from optimization tasks, clear immediate threat, then manually repair during a short lull or rely on Repair Post sustain if the affected structure remains powered
- Expected outcome: survivable with strain; front-line posture softens temporarily, but the defense should stabilize if the player reacts promptly
- Paper sim judgment: passes the intended recovery threshold

## Scenario B: One Destroyed Minor Support Piece

- Test event: one minor support piece such as a Shock Trap or Power Tap is destroyed during split pressure
- Player response: accept reduced safety, patch the exposed lane manually, and delay rebuild unless the destroyed piece is critical to front-line continuity
- Expected outcome: still survivable, but map stability degrades and the player loses margin for a second mistake
- Paper sim judgment: passes, but lands in the intended strain band rather than a clean comfort state

## Scenario C: Relay Node Destroyed

- Test event: a Relay Node is destroyed, taking part of the support chain offline while the front choke remains contested
- Player response: immediately shift from optimization to triage, protect the front with direct action, and choose between rebuilding the node or holding manually through the next contact
- Expected outcome: conditionally survivable only if the disabled chain is narrow and the player can cover the lost function with movement, repair timing, or a remaining side answer
- Paper sim judgment: borderline; this is the first clear place where a hidden fail cascade can appear if one Relay Node controls too much of the support space

## Scenario D: Repair Post Destroyed

- Test event: the Repair Post is destroyed during or just before Bruiser contact, removing passive sustain from the main hold
- Player response: compensate with manual repair windows, prioritize the most pressured structure, and abandon low-value optimization tasks completely until the contact stabilizes
- Expected outcome: survivable if the front choke remains structurally intact and the player is not simultaneously answering a second backline failure
- Paper sim judgment: passes with heavy strain; this supports the intended lesson that support sustain matters but is not the whole defense by itself

## Scenario E: Relay Node and Repair Post Both Lost

- Test event: a Relay Node and Repair Post are both lost within the same pressure window while the player is also containing Bruiser contact at the front
- Player response: forced into pure emergency play, usually choosing between preserving the choke and restoring support function rather than doing both adequately
- Expected outcome: probable spiral unless the player had surplus investment, unusually favorable pathing, or a nearly cleared front before the disruption occurred
- Paper sim judgment: this should fail often, but not instantly; if it becomes immediate unavoidable defeat, the support chain is too binary

## Simulation Readout

- Result: the locked minimum build state and support durability package generally produce the intended one-failure recovery pressure
- Primary concern: Relay Node dependency is the likeliest hidden fail cascade source because it can convert one breach into multi-system shutdown too quickly
- Secondary concern: if Power Taps and Relay Nodes overlap responsibility on the same route, one Sapper success may carry too much consequence for a minimum-state player
- Overall judgment: close to target, but requires explicit map-level support-chain limits to avoid binary collapse behavior

## Recommended Follow-Up Adjustments

- Limit each Relay Node to a small local support cluster rather than a large shared chain
- Avoid placing Power Tap and Relay Node as stacked single points of failure on the same exposed route in the minimum-path version of the sector
- If first playtests show instant collapse after one Relay loss, first reduce dependency spread before raising raw health values
- If first playtests show support disruption barely matters, lower support health slightly or increase the temporary penalties from offline support

## Locked Support-Chain Layout Constraints

- Purpose: prevent one breach from disabling too much of the defensive ecosystem at once, while keeping support routing meaningful and worth protecting.

## Core Constraints

- Each Relay Node may support only a small local cluster, not a large shared chain across multiple pressure zones
- Default cluster limit: one Relay Node should serve no more than 2 to 3 meaningful support dependencies in the minimum-path layout
- A Power Tap should power a local pocket only; it should not also be the upstream enabler for another exposed support bottleneck
- Critical front-line holding pieces must not all depend on the same Relay Node or the same Power Tap
- At least one meaningful lane answer should remain functional after any single support-node loss in the minimum-state layout
- Dependency intent: support routing should create triage decisions, not a one-hit full-system shutdown

## Wave 3 Minimum-State Rules

- The minimum Wave 3 path may include one exposed Relay Node and one exposed Power Tap, but they must not both gate the same support cluster
- If a side lane depends on support infrastructure, that lane must retain either a manual fallback route or an independent low-tier defensive answer after a single support failure
- The main choke may benefit from support sustain, but its entire stopping power cannot disappear from one backline support kill
- A player who loses one support node should still have a realistic movement path to intervene before the remaining defense collapses
- Wave 3 layout success rule: one support breach creates instability, not instant map invalidation

## Failure-Stacking Prohibitions

- Do not stack Relay Node and Power Tap as sequential single points of failure on the same exposed route in the minimum-state version
- Do not allow one support kill to simultaneously remove sustain, power, and lane control from the same contested space
- Do not place two critical support nodes so close together that a single unnoticed leak naturally destroys both during the same response window
- Do not require a full support-chain rebuild during one active Wave 3 contact for the minimum-state player to have any chance of survival
- Prohibition intent: fail states should come from compounded pressure and poor triage, not invisible dependency math

## Readability and Access Rules

- Support nodes that matter to Wave 3 survival must be readable from the player’s normal operating routes or from a quick response detour
- A Sapper path to critical support should be interceptable and legible, not a hidden inevitability through off-camera space
- Travel time from the main work loop to a threatened support cluster must be short enough for a competent player to respond after noticing the breach
- If a support cluster is intentionally remote, it must provide proportionally lower failure consequence than a central exposed cluster
- Readability intent: disruption should feel like a visible problem to solve, not a hidden condition discovered only after the defense has already failed

## Layout Tuning Triggers

- If one support-node kill still causes immediate unavoidable collapse, reduce dependency breadth before increasing durability
- If support disruption becomes too easy to ignore, increase local dependency importance or slightly reduce support durability
- If players repeatedly fail to identify which node matters, improve route legibility and support-state feedback before changing numbers
- Default tuning priority: fix support topology first, then durability, then enemy burst access

## Wave 3 Support Topology Sketch

- Purpose: translate the locked support-chain constraints into a playable Wave 3 layout where one breach causes triage pressure, but not full-system shutdown.

## Layout Overview

- Wave 3 uses three local support clusters instead of one shared chain: main choke hold, side-lane catch, and backline utility pocket
- The player’s main operating route sits between Cluster A and Cluster C, with a short response branch to Cluster B
- No single support node governs all three clusters
- The front line remains the visual priority, but support threats are placed close enough to the work loop to be noticed and answered

## Cluster A: Main Choke Hold

- Role: stop the primary Bruiser and Drone pressure long enough for the player to preserve the factory loop
- Core pieces: 1 Barrier, 1 Auto Turret, 1 Repair Post
- Support source: one local Power Tap only
- Critical rule: the Barrier and Auto Turret may share this power source, but the entire choke must not also require a Relay Node to remain functionally alive
- Failure target: if the Repair Post is lost, the choke becomes harder to hold; if the Power Tap is lost, at least one manual fallback or non-powered obstruction should still buy response time

## Cluster B: Side-Lane Catch

- Role: catch leaks, Sappers, or light split pressure before they reach the backline
- Core pieces: 1 Shock Trap plus either 1 side Barrier or 1 manual firing position with good sightlines
- Support source: one small Relay Node cluster with no more than 2 meaningful dependencies
- Critical rule: this Relay Node may support the Shock Trap and one minor sensor or door function, but not the main choke sustain or primary turret
- Failure target: if the Relay Node is destroyed, leaks become more dangerous and demand player movement, but the map should not instantly spiral

## Cluster C: Backline Utility Pocket

- Role: sustain local logistics or utility function without determining whether the whole defense lives or dies
- Core pieces: 1 Processor or logistics utility point, 1 minor powered helper if needed
- Support source: independent local power or a separate low-consequence support path
- Critical rule: backline utility may suffer slowdown when disrupted, but must not remove the player’s only surviving lane answer
- Failure target: utility disruption should hurt economy or convenience first, not instantly erase defensive agency

## Dependency Map

- Cluster A depends on: local Power Tap, local Repair Post, no Relay dependency required for baseline hold
- Cluster B depends on: one Relay Node, one local trap answer, optional manual support from player position
- Cluster C depends on: separate local utility support with low collapse consequence
- Safe loss model: losing any one cluster weakens either hold quality, leak control, or logistics efficiency, but does not remove all three at once
- Unsafe model to avoid: Relay Node loss disables side catch, main sustain, and utility output in one event

## Sapper Access and Intercept Windows

- Sapper access to Cluster B should exist through a readable flank route that crosses at least one player-visible approach line
- Sapper access to Cluster A should be rarer and more telegraphed than access to Cluster B
- Cluster C disruption should come from overflow or missed leaks, not from a direct hidden shortcut behind the player
- Intercept rule: a competent player must be able to reach Cluster B fast enough to save it after first noticing a breach
- Pressure intent: Cluster B should be the most contestable support pocket, because it teaches disruption response without instantly deleting the main hold

## Expected Failure Behavior

- If Cluster B fails, the player faces more leaks and higher attention load, but the main choke still functions
- If Cluster A loses only the Repair Post, the player can still hold by rotating repairs manually
- If Cluster A loses power, the hold should degrade sharply but still buy a brief manual intervention window
- If Cluster C fails, the economy slows or rerouting becomes awkward, but survival remains primarily determined by Clusters A and B
- Target behavior: one cluster failure creates pressure, two cluster failures create probable spiral, three cluster failures produce expected collapse

## Sector Planner Notes

- Place Cluster B near a branch off the player’s normal refinement or transport route so support response competes with factory attention in a readable way
- Keep Cluster A visually central and Cluster C slightly recessed so the player intuitively understands survival priority
- Do not mirror all support pockets symmetrically; one intentionally contestable side pocket is enough for Wave 3
- Wave 3 topology priority: teach layered support defense, not full network optimization complexity

## Wave 3 Lane-by-Lane Placement Draft

- Purpose: place the three support clusters into a readable Wave 3 map flow so support pressure remains visible, interceptable, and non-binary.

## Macro Route Structure

- The player’s normal work loop should run from Backline Utility Loop through a central transit spine into the Main Attack Corridor
- The Side Intercept Branch should split off that central spine at a visible junction before the player reaches the main choke
- The player should repeatedly pass the branch decision point during normal upkeep so support threats compete with front-line attention in a readable way
- No lane should require an isolated dead-end trek so long that support defense feels disconnected from the main play loop

## Lane 1: Main Attack Corridor

- Cluster A sits at the far end of the main corridor as the visual anchor of Wave 3 defense
- The main choke should be visible from the central spine before full commitment, so the player can assess whether the hold is stable while still able to pivot
- Place the local Power Tap slightly behind or offset from the Barrier line so it is attackable, but not incidentally destroyed by every front-line breach
- Place the Repair Post within one short movement beat of the choke so players can rotate repairs without abandoning the lane completely
- Reserve at least one manual fallback object, corner, or obstruction near the choke that still slows pressure after power loss

## Lane 2: Side Intercept Branch

- Cluster B should sit on a side branch that is near enough to the central spine to be checked quickly, but far enough that ignoring it has real cost
- The branch entrance should be readable from the player’s normal route, with some visibility into whether enemies are leaking through it
- Place the Relay Node inside Cluster B, not behind Cluster A, so side-lane disruption remains local and legible
- The Shock Trap should cover the branch approach before enemies fully enter the backline, creating an intercept pocket rather than a last-second save
- The player should be able to run from the central branch junction to Cluster B in one short response sprint
- A competent player arriving promptly should usually be able to save either the Relay Node or the leak-control piece, even if not both

## Lane 3: Backline Utility Loop

- Cluster C should sit behind the central spine, slightly recessed from the main corridor, but not fully hidden from player traffic
- The utility pocket should connect naturally to the player’s production or transport maintenance route
- Do not place Cluster C behind a hidden shortcut that lets enemies delete it without first threatening a readable route
- If enemies reach Cluster C, the player should already have had earlier warning through Side Intercept failure, leak overflow, or visible route breach
- Utility damage should feel like a consequence of missed containment, not a primary stealth kill condition

## Cluster Placement Summary

- Cluster A: placed at the end of the main corridor, powered locally, readable from central approach
- Cluster B: placed on the side branch near the central junction, Relay dependency localized, highly contestable
- Cluster C: placed in the recessed utility loop, tied to logistics rather than primary survival gating
- The central junction is the key teaching space: it should force repeated choose-frontline-or-support decisions during Wave 3

## Sightline and Readability Rules

- The player should be able to see signs of trouble at Cluster B from the branch entrance or central spine, even if the full pocket is not constantly visible
- The player should be able to visually reassess Cluster A while moving toward or away from the junction
- Cluster C can be slightly less exposed visually, but must remain integrated into normal traversal so its state is not forgotten
- Do not let geometry block all awareness of a support breach until after lane failure has already propagated

## Response-Time Targets

- Central spine to Cluster A: moderate run, because the main choke should feel committed and important
- Central junction to Cluster B: short sprint, because side support must be realistically defendable after first notice
- Central spine to Cluster C: very short detour, because upkeep and utility checking should be naturally folded into the work loop
- If travel time to Cluster B is long enough that the player almost never saves it after noticing danger, the branch is too remote
- If travel time to Cluster A is so short that front-line commitment disappears, the main corridor has lost its weight

## Placement Failure Checks

- Failure check: can one Sapper route threaten both Cluster B Relay and Cluster A power in the same unnoticed pass? If yes, separate them further
- Failure check: does ignoring Cluster B for one response window make Cluster C collapse automatically? If yes, reduce path coupling
- Failure check: can the player notice trouble at Cluster B before the branch is already lost? If no, improve sightline or alert readability
- Failure check: after Cluster A power loss, is there still a brief playable fallback beat? If no, add obstruction or reduce dependency severity
- Failure check: does Cluster C matter enough to care about, but not enough to binary-gate survival? Keep it in that middle band

## Sector Planner Build Notes

- Build the central junction first, because that space determines whether Wave 3 support decisions feel readable or annoying
- Then place Cluster B and test save windows before polishing Cluster C, because side-branch fairness is the highest topology risk
- Place Cluster A power slightly safer than Cluster B Relay, but more consequential when lost
- Wave 3 placement priority: preserve player read-response-save loops, not perfect geometric symmetry

## Wave 3 Cluster B Sapper Route Spec

- Purpose: define the exact Sapper attack route into Cluster B so the branch remains contestable, readable, and locally disruptive without causing hidden front-line shutdown.

## Route Identity

- Use one primary Sapper route into Cluster B: a readable flank path that enters from the side of the branch rather than from the main front or a hidden backdoor
- The route should feel like a pressure lane the player could have anticipated, not a surprise teleport into support infrastructure
- This route exists to test response discipline at Cluster B, not to create direct access to Cluster A power or Cluster C utility in the same pass

## Path Beats

- Beat 1: Entry lane. The Sapper appears on a visible side approach line that can be seen from either the branch entrance or a nearby central-spine sightline
- Beat 2: Contest pocket. The route narrows through the Shock Trap zone or a nearby side cover pocket where the player can intercept before the Sapper reaches the Relay Node
- Beat 3: Relay threat zone. If unopposed, the Sapper reaches the Relay Node and begins the disruption attempt there first
- Beat 4: Leak continuation. After disabling or damaging the Relay Node, surviving pressure spills deeper past Cluster B toward leak-control failure rather than jumping directly to backline utility destruction
- Do not allow the route to bypass the contest pocket and instantly touch the Relay Node on first contact

## Detection and Visibility Windows

- First detection window: the player should be able to notice the Sapper during Beat 1 from a normal movement line, without needing a dedicated watch position
- Second detection window: if the player misses the initial sighting, the contest pocket should produce a strong visual or combat cue before Relay contact
- Relay contact should be the third awareness moment, not the first
- If the map only communicates the threat after the Relay is already disabled, the route is unfair and must be revised

## Target Priority

- Primary target: Cluster B Relay Node
- Secondary target: Shock Trap or side leak-control piece if the Relay is defended or already down
- Forbidden target path: Cluster A Power Tap should not be the natural next hop from this route
- Forbidden target path: Cluster C utility should only be threatened after leak overflow, not via direct Sapper priority on the first branch breach

## Player Save Window

- Fast response outcome: player spots the Sapper during Beat 1 or Beat 2 and can usually save both the Relay Node and the leak-control piece
- Average response outcome: player reacts during late Beat 2 and can usually save either the Relay Node or the leak-control piece, but not always both
- Late response outcome: player reacts after Relay contact and can contain spillover, but should normally suffer Cluster B disruption first
- This timing model is correct for Wave 3 because it rewards awareness and movement without demanding perfection

## Failure Boundaries

- Allowed consequence: one missed Sapper response disables Cluster B support and raises leak pressure
- Not allowed: one missed Sapper response also disables Cluster A baseline hold
- Not allowed: the Sapper route reaches Cluster C directly before the player has a visible chance to contain the branch failure
- If one route can cause side-catch loss, front-line collapse, and utility deletion in one short sequence, the topology has over-coupled and must be split

## Sector Planner Notes

- Test this route first with no extra alerts beyond geometry readability; if players still fail unfairly, fix spatial communication before adding UI compensation
- Keep the route singular and legible in Wave 3; branching multi-entry Sapper patterns should wait for later escalation
- Wave 3 teaching goal: the player learns to read a flank, rotate quickly, and preserve a local support cluster under pressure

## Wave 3 Cluster B Intercept Window Spec

- Purpose: define the response timing model for the Cluster B Sapper route so support defense produces fair save windows instead of hidden binary losses

## Timing Model

- Start timing at first fair notice, not at spawn time. If the player cannot reasonably perceive the Sapper yet, the intercept clock has not meaningfully started
- Use three route timing segments: Notice Window, Contest Window, and Relay Contact Window
- Notice Window: from first fair visibility at Beat 1 until the Sapper fully enters the contest pocket
- Contest Window: from contest-pocket entry until first meaningful Relay contact attempt
- Relay Contact Window: from first Relay threat until support disruption is effectively secured if uncontested

## Response Bands

- Fast response band: player notices during the Notice Window and commits immediately
- Average response band: player notices late in the Notice Window or early in the Contest Window and rotates with a normal decision delay
- Late response band: player notices only during the Contest Window or at Relay contact and arrives after local damage has begun
- Missed response band: player never rotates until after Relay disruption and leak escalation are already underway

## Expected Outcomes by Arrival Time

- Fast response outcome: player should usually preserve the Relay Node, preserve the local leak-control piece, and clear the threat with manageable front-line opportunity cost
- Average response outcome: player should usually preserve either the Relay Node or the leak-control piece, but accept some local degradation or pressure spill
- Late response outcome: player should usually lose Relay integrity or local support function first, but still retain a meaningful containment play before Cluster C becomes threatened
- Missed response outcome: Cluster B disruption is secured, leak pressure rises, and the player is pushed into recovery mode rather than instant map death

## Travel and Notice Assumptions

- Assume the player is on a normal upkeep or front-support loop near the central spine when first fair notice occurs
- Assume the run from the central junction to Cluster B is a short response sprint, not a major redeployment
- Assume a small but real human decision delay between notice and movement, because players do not pivot instantly on every signal
- Assume the player may arrive while also weighing the cost of leaving Cluster A under temporary strain; this tradeoff is intended and should remain

## Tuning Thresholds

- If fast responses still frequently fail to save the Relay Node, the intercept window is too short or the first fair notice is too weak
- If average responses often save everything with little tradeoff, the intercept window is too generous and the branch loses tension
- If late responses cause immediate Cluster C deletion, consequence propagation is too steep and needs another buffer stage
- If missed responses collapse both Cluster B and the main hold in the same short sequence, the side branch is over-coupled to front-line survival

## Playtest Validation Rules

- Validate with blind route tests first: do not add extra UI warnings until geometry and route staging have been judged on their own
- A fair Wave 3 result means most attentive players land in the average band, strong players often hit the fast band, and inattentive players drift into late or missed bands
- If nearly all players land in late or missed bands, improve branch readability or reduce time-to-Relay rather than compensating with bigger rewards
- If nearly all players land in fast band with no meaningful cost, the branch is under-pressuring and should demand harder commitment or slightly weaker early visibility

- Wave 1 teaches shape and cleanup.

- Wave 3 teaches split attention and support-line vulnerability.

# Human-Scale Audit + FP Embodiment (F13, 2026-07-27)

The ship's geometry was authored to read from the 14 m iso camera and had never been checked against
a 1.65 m eye. Measured in Play against that eye; the headline is that **the deck passes and nothing
about the ship was resized** — the one real outlier is the player character, recorded below.

Measured (Play, eye 1.65):
- Props sit at human scale: desk 0.79, barrel 0.78, table 0.89, bench 1.21, console 1.53,
  locker 1.85. A worker reads correctly against every one of these.
- Ceiling lid at y ~3.07-3.2 = 1.94x eye. Industrial-plausible (real plant decks run 3-4 m); reads
  as a lid overhead in first person, not a cathedral.
- Corridors measure 6.0 m clear between wall faces. Wider than a real ship passage, but this is
  **deliberately not changed**: the 2026-07-21 human Decision forbids pulling the walls in, and the
  width is what lets belts, machines and lanes share a corridor. Factory layout is the primary skill
  expression; narrowing it would cost the loop more than it would buy in atmosphere.
- Gate mouths ~4.0 tall x 7.0 wide including the P6 airlock frame — sized for lane throughput.

Caveat worth remembering: a raycast up from the deck reports **no ceiling**, because the lid and most
dressing are collider-free. Renderer bounds are the correct instrument (207 ceiling renderers spanning
121.9 x 81.6). Do not "fix" a missing ceiling that is only missing from the physics query.

Known outlier, NOT fixed here: the player body renders ~2.26 m tall (126% of a 1.8 m human), so in
iso the worker slightly towers over a 1.85 m locker, and the 1.65 eye sits at ~73% of body height
where a human's is ~93%. This is invisible in first person (the body is hidden there), so it is an
iso silhouette / lore-fantasy issue rather than an FP scale one. Left alone deliberately: shrinking
the actor also shrinks its iso readability at 14 m, which is a preference call rather than a bug.
Logged to the backlog Ice box.

FP embodiment (`FirstPersonCamera.HeadMotion`):
- Walk head motion is a LOCAL camera offset added to (never replacing) the CameraShake term, so hits
  and impacts still punch through the gait.
- Full-walk values, measured over a sustained 4.50 m/s run: vertical travel 0.044 m peak-to-peak,
  lateral 0.022 m, ~3.1 Hz (vertical runs at twice the sway frequency — two footfalls per cycle).
  For comparison a conventional FPS bob is 0.10-0.15 m; this is deliberately about a third of that.
- Amplitude scales with actual horizontal speed and eases (not the phase), so stopping mid-stride
  settles the head instead of snapping it to centre. Standing still leaves only a ~0.006 m breath.
- `headMotion = false` on the component disables it entirely as a motion-sickness fallback.
- Footstep audio is deliberately absent: that is SND7, behind the closed audio gate.
- Iso is untouched — the whole routine sits behind LateUpdate's first-person guard.

# Threatened-Lane Lamp Death (L27, 2026-07-27)

The lamp over the approach that is about to be used dies first, so the player can read which
corridor to go stand in without a HUD marker. Diegetic dread from `lore/BIBLE.md`: lights die and
rooms get blacker as the ship loses ground, and pressure is read off the world, not off a meter.

Rules:
- Armed during **late Prep** (AlarmLevel >= 0.55, i.e. the wave is nearly here) and for the whole
  **fight**. Recovery and early prep restore the fixture, so the beat reads as pressure arriving and
  then passing.
- Exactly **one** fixture, the live lamp nearest the threatened lane's mouth (within 18 m).
  Threatened lane = VentBreach when the wave actually assigned spawns there, otherwise WestCorridor
  as the teaching arc expects.
- Fixtures within 12 m of the hub are never darkened — the player has to keep building and reading
  the factory floor while a lane goes dark. Measured in play: 1 dark, 6 lit.
- Deliberately distinct from the HorrorClock zone decay, which kills a *fraction* of the VentBreach
  zone as a long-run scar. This is a single, temporary, directional cue.

Ownership note for future agents: `HorrorClock` owns `LampFlicker.forceDead` and rewrites it every
tick for its zone lamps. L27 therefore owns a **separate** flag, `LampFlicker.laneThreatDead`, and a
lamp is dark if `IsDark` (either flag) is set. Do not add a second writer to `forceDead`; L27 also
skips any fixture the clock has already killed, since darkening an already-dark lamp is not a
telegraph the player can see.

Verified in play: combat on the west lane put the mouth fixture at (-40, 3, 0) to intensity 0.000
with 6 lamps still lit; after the wave cleared (Prep, alarm 0.00) the same fixture returned to 3.240.

# Shift Schedule Board (L28, 2026-07-27)

A steel/amber board beside the hub posts the roster in flat scheduling language while the deck is
being breached. From `lore/BIBLE.md`: the company built the hostile workplace the hive inherited, and
its paperwork never notices — the board reports the same catastrophe the player is living through as
a routine status change, which is the joke and the dread at once.

Two terse lines, no cheer, no exclamation, no screen chrome:
- `SHIFT nn` — tracks the wave number, so it ticks up across a run.
- Status: `PREP WINDOW` → `BREACH ACTIVE` (any non-Prep phase) → `CLEAR - RESUME DUTY` for 9 s after
  a wave clears, then back to `PREP WINDOW`. The stand-down line is deliberately time-boxed: the
  paperwork acknowledges the catastrophe for exactly as long as required and then resumes scheduling.

Built from primitives + `TextMesh` in the same grammar as the L25 `SectorPlaques` (steel body, amber
emissive edge, `ShipTerminalUI` font) so it reads from the iso camera and in first person without a
canvas. Sits at hub + (3.0, −2.6), clear of the L25 hub plaque at (−2.6, −2.6); own child root, never
on the shared SectorRuntime object.

Implementation note: the board compares the *inputs* (shift number, breach, recovering) rather than
the composed string before rewriting the label — composing every poll just to discover nothing changed
would allocate a string four times a second for a sign that changes a handful of times per run.

Verified in play across the real wave path: `SHIFT 01 | PREP WINDOW` → `SHIFT 01 | BREACH ACTIVE` →
`SHIFT 01 | CLEAR - RESUME DUTY` → back to `PREP WINDOW`, and later `SHIFT 02 | BREACH ACTIVE` once
wave 2 began — legible in an iso capture beside the hub.

# Far-Deck Labour Fill (L32, 2026-07-28)

Emptiness becomes industrialization. The 2026-07-21 Decision rules that the empty deck is expansion
headroom, not a bug, so it is FILLED as the run goes on — never shrunk. `FarDeckLabourFill` adds small
labour clusters (Synty crates, barrels, power cells, greebles) onto open deck as the operation grows.

- Budget = `WavesCleared * 2 + PoweredProducers`, capped at **24** props. The deck should look
  increasingly worked, never carpeted. It only ever ADDS — a shrinking factory does not remove props.
- Keep-outs: **16 m** from the hub (the nest pass already owns that ground) and **3 m** from every
  lane. Colliders are stripped, so dressing can never block pathing or narrow a walkway.
- Placement is a deterministic sweep, so a given run always fills the same way and a later pass
  continues where the last one stopped instead of re-rolling the deck.
- Rejects anything overlapping authored wall geometry by **renderer bounds, not a raycast** — the hull
  and most dressing are collider-free, so a physics query reports clear air inside a wall.

Why a standalone runtime component and not a pass inside `PlaceholderPropDressing`: that dresser
**never runs in the hand-authored sector** (`SectorAuthoring` skips `AddGeometryDressing`), so
extending it would have shipped dead code that silently did nothing. `SectorRuntimeBootstrap` adds
`FarDeckLabourFill` directly. Check which dressers actually execute before extending one.

Asset note: the L32 task text said "primitives only", written while the asset pack was unpurchased.
The pack is open and P7 made every other deck prop Synty, so primitives would now read as a bug.

Verified in play: a fresh run placed 7; with the operation grown to 14 powered producers it filled to
14 (cap 24), with `inLane=0` (nearest 3.42 m), `nearHub=0` (min 16.3 m), `colliders=0`, and nothing
outside the play area. Map bounds unchanged. The far deck is dim by design — these props read as
low-contrast shapes out there rather than lit set dressing, which is the F7/F8 lighting question, not
this one.
